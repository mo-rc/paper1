# Amendment A7 (2026-11-17, before any RL run): tuning and pilot evaluation — budgets, sets and raw metrics

Draft text, proposed for recording in `docs/spec.md`. Status: **proposed** (plan only: no code, no runs, nothing committed, spec.md not edited). On approval it is recorded in the A5/A6 form — date, "before any RL run", the superseded sentences kept visible in place. A7 does not change the checker, the physics, the objective, the splits or their hashes, or the success criteria. Companion plan: `docs/p12a_plan.md`.

## 1. The problem

A5 fixes the tuning budget (READ: `docs/spec.md` line 379): 12 configurations per entry, one seed, "3,000 checker calls per configuration, counting every call it makes, including the verification of the 1,000 V contexts (about 2,000 calls of learning)", and "A pilot run counts its 10,000 training calls plus 1,000 V verification calls". That arithmetic assumes one call per context, which holds only for F0/F2.

**(a) F1 evaluation.** A greedy F1 rollout is one reset call plus at most N_MAX = 13 step visits (VERIFIED: `src/ub_rl/reward_checks.py:32`), so at most 14 calls per context. Evaluating 1,000 V contexts costs up to 14,000 calls; at the P7-measured heuristic mean of 14.19 calls per context on V (READ: `docs/verification_record.md`, P7 full-run table, val row) about 14,190 calls — more than the 10,000-call pilot budget by itself and far above 3,000. The A5 rule is unsatisfiable for F1 as written (the P11 open item; READ: `docs/verification_record.md` P11 entry, `docs/p11_plan.md` section 2).

**(b) Repair is unbounded.** The P11 harness repairs every infeasible evaluation output by stepping up in mass order (VERIFIED: `src/ub_rl/harness.py:246-272`). An untrained F0/F2 policy can output the lightest section of C in any context; repair then walks up to 90 further sections (C has 91 rows; READ: `docs/spec.md` section 4) — up to 90 repair calls per context, up to 90,000 on 1,000 V contexts. Counting repair on V inside a 3,000-call budget is impossible for every entry.

## 2. Decisions

**D1 (the problem, fixed as stated above).** A7 supersedes the A5 arithmetic where it assumes one call per context and no repair.

**D2 — No repair executed in tuning and pilots.** During tuning and pilots the checkpoint evaluation executes NO repair and makes no repair call: F0/F2 evaluation costs 1 call per context (the greedy sample's checker call is the verification); F1 costs its rollout calls. Selection uses raw verified feasibility (the greedy output passes every check) and the offline repaired gap of D5; the raw mean gap over feasible outputs is kept as a reported column. End-to-end repaired gap and per-design cost with executed, counted repair are reported only for the P13 runs (separate evaluation, `budget_includes_eval` false); section 10's per-task cost (READ: `docs/spec.md` line 152) is unchanged. Reason: repair is unbounded (D1b) and would let evaluation cost swamp any fixed budget; raw feasibility stays the primary signal, and the repaired gap still enters selection — computed offline in D5, at zero RL cost.

**D3 — Evaluation sets.** F0/F2: all 1,000 V contexts. F1: a fixed 100-context V subset — the first 100 contexts of `val.csv` in file order ("val:100", already supported by the harness; VERIFIED: `src/ub_rl/harness.py:112-113, 207-215`). Before the subset is used, P12a runs a **descriptive** representativeness check of the first 100 against all 1,000 (feasible share, F1 trial-section index distribution, F1 distance to the optimum) from the committed P7/P9 tables or cheap checker evaluations that are NOT RL budget; the result is recorded. The subset stays the first 100 either way; the check is descriptive, not a gate. Worst-case F1 evaluation cost: 100 × 14 = 1,400 calls. Learning calls that remain: F1 at least 3,000 − 1,400 = 1,600 (tuning; the reserve is at most 1,400, the difference recorded as `unused_calls`), F0/F2 2,000 (their evaluation is exactly 1 call per context); pilots keep their full 10,000 training calls under A5's reading (D4), with the evaluation calls on top and counted into T. Reason: 1,000 F1 rollouts cannot fit the registered budgets; 100 keeps F1 evaluation inside them while keeping the registered selection protocol.

**D4 — Budget reading: tuning total, pilots as A5.** Tuning: the 3,000-call budget is a **total** including evaluation (`budget_includes_eval` true). The checkpoint threshold is 3,000 − the evaluation reserve for every entry: **2,000 for F0/F2 — exactly 2,000 calls of learning —** and **1,600 for F1** (the reserve is at most 1,400, the difference recorded as `unused_calls`). The harness fires checkpoints after every step (`fire_checkpoints()` after each `agent.update()`, which returns None when the batch is not full; READ: `src/ub_rl/harness.py:373-381`), so the threshold overshoot is 0 calls for F0/F2 and at most 1 for F1, independent of batch_size. The P12a driver asserts total calls ≤ 3,000 on every tuning run; a failed assertion marks the run failed, not complete. Pilots keep A5's reading: 10,000 **training** calls (`budget_includes_eval` false), checkpoint threshold 10,000 with the actual training-call count recorded, and the evaluation on top at the 10,000-call checkpoint counted into T — 10,000 + 1,000 for F0/F2, at most 10,000 + 1,400 for F1. Reason: the tuning sentence's own arithmetic ("about 2,000 calls of learning" from 3,000) already reads that budget as total including evaluation; the pilot sentence already separates training from verification, so it is kept.

**D5 — Selection rule.** The section 9 rule is kept: a change counts only if validation mean gap improves by at least 0.5 percentage points with feasibility not lower (READ: `docs/spec.md` line 138). **Incumbent:** the entry's provisional default configuration (`configs/f0_r1.json`, `f1_r1.json`, `f2_r3.json`; READ, P11 marked them provisional), evaluated under the identical A7 protocol (same seed, same 3,000-call total budget, same evaluation set, no repair); it is always configuration 1 of the 12, never a random draw. **Metrics:** feasibility = D2's raw verified feasibility over the feasible contexts of the entry's evaluation set. Gap = the **end-to-end repaired gap computed offline** from the V enumeration (the P6 audit labels; no RL call executed or counted, never visible to the policy): the registered mass-order repair of the greedy output is applied to the enumeration labels, and the gap is the mean over the feasible contexts **where the repair succeeds**; repair abstentions are reported as counts. The raw mean gap over feasible outputs is a reported column, not a selection metric. **Comparison:** a configuration passes iff raw feasibility ≥ the incumbent's AND repaired gap ≤ the incumbent's − 0.5 percentage points. Repaired feasibility (the share of feasible contexts where the offline repair finds a section) is reported; a candidate whose repaired feasibility is below the incumbent's cannot pass (feasibility after repair below 1 already fails A5's N* condition at P13, READ: `docs/spec.md` line 387). This absorbs the earlier no-feasible-output edge case: a candidate whose repair never finds a section has repaired feasibility 0 and no repaired gap, so it cannot pass. **Why offline repair:** the raw gap averages over each candidate's own feasible outputs, so the gap set differs when raw feasibility differs and a repair-free raw gap can never reward feasibility gains (infeasible outputs contribute no gap); the repaired gap scores every candidate on its repaired outputs and rewards feasibility gains through repair (a feasible output is scored at its own mass, an infeasible one at the repaired heavier section). **Among passers:** highest raw feasibility, ties by lowest repaired gap, further ties keep the incumbent. **None passes:** the incumbent (the provisional default) stays. All 12 configurations are run and recorded.

**D6 — R-C trigger reading.** A5: "if, in the pilots on V, no F0, F1 or F2 entry reaches raw top-1 verified feasibility of at least 0.99 in every pilot seed, R-C is run on the best F1 entry; R-C's constants are frozen at Gate 4" (READ: `docs/spec.md` line 385). Exact reading: the pilots are the P12a pilots (3 seeds × 10,000-call total budget, provisional defaults, V only); the evaluation is at the pilot's 10,000-call checkpoint, under A7 (no repair, greedy output); "raw top-1 verified feasibility" is D2's raw feasibility — the greedy output was checker-verified during the rollout, with no repair applied — over the feasible contexts of the entry's A7 evaluation set (all 1,000 V for F0/F2; the 100-context subset for F1). An entry reaches the bar iff **all three** of its pilot seeds are ≥ 0.99. On the F1 set the bar is exacting: 71 of the first 100 V contexts are feasible (VERIFIED by a non-RL checker enumeration of the first 100 `val.csv` rows, 9,100 checker calls, 2026-11-18; the P12a representativeness check reproduces and records it) — at n = 71 the 0.99 bar means **zero misses** (0.99 × 71 = 70.29, so all 71 feasible contexts must be correct), i.e. F1 meets the pilot bar only if the greedy output is feasible on every feasible context of the subset. If at least one entry reaches it, the trigger does not fire and R-C is not run; if none does, R-C runs on the best F1 entry (the A7-selected F1 configuration) and its constants are frozen at Gate 4 (P12b). The trigger stays a pilot-based early warning only: an S1 failure at P13 reports RL as not meeting the criteria, no rescue run (A5, unchanged). Reason: A5 names the pilots as the trigger's only input; a pilot has exactly one evaluation point; "every pilot seed" is all three seeds of an entry; the A7 metrics are the pilot metrics.

**D7 — Effects and limitations (stated honestly).** (i) F1 selection on 100 contexts is noisier than on 1,000 (standard error of a feasibility share ≈ 0.01 at 0.99 on 100 contexts versus ≈ 0.003 on 1,000), and at 71 feasible contexts the 0.99 bar admits no miss (D6). (ii) F1's tuning leaves ≈ 1,600 training calls — about 160 episodes at ~10 steps — so the selected F1 configuration reflects early learning speed, not 200,000-call behaviour. (iii) Single-seed 3,000-call tuning is noisy: the selected configuration can win by seed-0 luck; seed spread exists only in the pilots (3 seeds, default config only) and at P13 (10 seeds). (iv) The offline repaired gap is a selection metric only; the end-to-end repaired numbers with executed, counted repair are P13's, unchanged (repair raises feasibility to 1.0 by construction and changes the gap only by stepping up). (v) No change to the splits or hashes, checker, physics, objective, success criteria, the 0.5-pp / feasibility-not-lower thresholds, N* and N_ref = 10,000, or the ten-seed statistics.
